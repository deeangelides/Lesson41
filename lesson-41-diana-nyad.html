A big dream

Diana Nyad is an American long-distance swimmer who was born in 1949. As a young woman, she had an enormous dream: to swim all the way from Cuba to Florida — a distance of about 110 miles through open ocean. She first tried in 1978, when she was just 28 years old, but rough seas and strong currents pushed her off course, and after many hours she had to climb out of the water.

Trying again and again

Most people would have given up after that. Diana did not — but she did put her dream aside for a very long time. Then, as she neared the age of 60, she decided to try once more. She came back and attempted the swim again at the age of 61 — and then again, and again. Each time, something stopped her: fierce storms, powerful currents, circling sharks, and painful stings from venomous jellyfish. In all, she tried and failed four times. She had been chasing this one dream for over thirty years, but she still refused to quit.

Never giving up

Then came her fifth attempt. In 2013 — thirty-five years after she first began — everything finally came together. Wearing a special suit and mask to protect her from the jellyfish, Diana swam for almost 53 hours without stopping, right through the day and the night. At the age of 64, she staggered onto a beach in Key West, Florida, and became the first person ever to swim from Cuba to Florida without a shark cage. Her message to the world was simple: never, ever give up — and you are never too old to chase your dreams.

🤔 Section 4 · What are they?

Actions that keep going

The perfect continuous tenses describe an action that keeps going over a period of time.

Every one of them uses a form of be + an -ing verb:

has / have / had / will have+been+swimming
4Meet the Three Tensespast · present · future
⏪ Past

had been + -ing

was going on before another past moment.

“She had been training for years before she won.”

▶️ Present

has / have been + -ing

started in the past and is still going (or just stopped).

“She has been swimming for hours.”

⏩ Future

will have been + -ing

will have been going on up to a future point.

“By midnight, she will have been swimming for two days.”
